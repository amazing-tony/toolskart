Compound Interest Formula

Unlike simple interest, compound interest is calculated on the initial principal, which also includes all of the accumulated interest from previous periods.

A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt)

Where:
A = Total Amount (Principal + Interest)
P = Principal Amount
r = Annual Interest Rate (decimal)
n = Number of times interest is compounded per year
t = Time in years

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Simple and Compound Interest?

Simple interest is based solely on the principal amount. Compound interest is based on the principal amount and the interest that accumulates on it every period. Over time, compound interest grows much faster.

Why does compounding frequency matter?

More frequent compounding (e.g., monthly vs. yearly) means that interest is added to your balance sooner, allowing it to start earning its own interest earlier. This results in slightly higher total returns.
